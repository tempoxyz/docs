# Use cases

Find the setup and guides for what you're building.

Consumer apps, backends and agents, custodians and treasuries, and wallets each set up accounts differently. The difference is who holds which key: a customer's passkey, a provider's key, your backend's access key, or an approver's signer. Tempo limits each key to what the account allows, on every transaction.

Single-key accounts, access keys, fee sponsorship, virtual addresses, and receive policies work on mainnet and testnet. Configurable accounts, with several signers and a threshold, are planned for the [T14 network upgrade](/docs/protocol/upgrades/t14). Until then, they run only on the [Tempo Accounts devnet](/docs/accounts/create#connect-to-the-devnet).

## Customer accounts or a company account

Funds live in each customer's own account or in one company account. The choice applies to every balance your product holds:

| | Customer accounts | Company account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stablecoins** | Each customer's account holds their balance. | One account holds the total. Your ledger tracks each customer's share. |
| **Earn positions** | Each account holds its own vault shares. The Earn API reports earnings per address. | The company account holds the shares. Your ledger allocates the earnings. |
| **Zone balances** | Each customer reads only their own private balance. | One Zone account holds the funds. Your ledger tracks each customer's share. |

<Cards>

  <Card title="Company account" description="Give each customer a deposit address that pays into one omnibus account." to="/docs/accounts/custodians/omnibus-accounts" />

A customer's deposit address identifies incoming payments. It doesn't hold a separate balance or give the customer signing authority over the company account.

You can combine the two. Customers spend from their own accounts, while a company account holds Earn positions and pays out.
