# Use cases

Each use case gives every party its own key, and Tempo enforces what each key can do.

The difference between them is who holds which key. A customer's passkey, a company approver's signer, your backend's access key, and an agent's access key can each do only what the account allows them to do, and no one else can widen that.

## Consumer wallets

Your app gives each customer an account they control, even though your app and your wallet provider help run it. The customer's passkey approves payments on its own. Your wallet provider's key and a recovery key approve only together, for example to replace the passkey when the customer loses their phone, so neither you nor the provider can move funds alone.

An account with several signers like this is a configurable account, which runs only on the [Tempo Accounts devnet](/docs/accounts/create#connect-to-the-devnet). A provider-held single-key account works on mainnet today: see [Embedded wallets](/docs/accounts/wallets/embedded-wallets).

## Business accounts and treasuries

A company keeps its stablecoins in one account that no single person can spend from. Each approver holds their own signer, and any two of three must approve a payment. A team can spend from its own daily budget without asking the approvers each time, and when an approver leaves, you remove their signer without moving the funds.

Treasuries use configurable accounts, which run only on the [Tempo Accounts devnet](/docs/accounts/create#connect-to-the-devnet).

## Automated payments

Your backend pays from a customer's account without asking them each time: funding a card swipe, charging a subscription, sweeping idle balances into Earn, or sending a scheduled transfer. The customer approves one [access key](/docs/accounts/access-keys) for your backend, with a spending limit, a list of recipients, and an expiry. Tempo checks every payment against those bounds, so even a leaked backend key can't spend past them.

Access keys work on mainnet and testnet today.

## Agent wallets

An AI agent pays for what it needs, such as API calls, with its own access key: a budget, an expiry, and a list of the calls it may make. Tempo rejects anything outside those bounds, whatever the agent decides to do, and you can revoke the key at any time to stop it.

Access keys work on mainnet and testnet today.
