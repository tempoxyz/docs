# Choose an account model

Decide whether each customer needs a separate onchain balance or whether deposits should collect in a shared account. Then choose who can authorize payments from those accounts.

<span id="integration-walkthrough" />
<span id="associate-the-address-with-your-business-records" />

## Where should funds live?

Funds live in each customer's own account, or in a company account where your ledger records what each customer is owed. The same choice applies to every balance your product holds:

| Balance | Customer accounts | Company account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stablecoins** | Each customer's account holds its balance. Read it at that address. | One account holds the total. Your ledger records each customer's credit. |
| **[Earn](/docs/earn) positions** | Each customer's account holds its vault shares. The Earn API reports earnings per address. | The company account holds the shares. Your ledger allocates their earnings to customers. |
| **[Zone](/docs/zones) balances** | Each customer reads only their own private balance through account-scoped RPC. | One Zone account holds the funds. Your ledger is the per-customer record. |
| **Build it** | [Create an account](/docs/accounts/create) or [connect the customer's wallet](/docs/accounts/integrate) | [Customer deposit addresses](/docs/guide/payments/virtual-addresses) to identify deposits; [fund the account](/docs/guide/getting-funds) and [send payments](/docs/guide/payments/send-a-payment) for payouts |

A virtual deposit address identifies incoming payments; it does **not** hold a separate balance or give the customer signing authority over the shared account. For example, if two customers deposit 100 and 50 tokens, the shared account receives 150. Your ledger records which customer receives each credit.

You can combine these models: a product might give customers separate accounts for spending while a company account holds Earn positions and pays out.

## Who approves payments?

Separate accounts do not determine who controls signing. Choose signing based on how your product approves spending:

- **The customer approves each payment:** connect an existing wallet, or use an [embedded wallet provider](/docs/accounts/providers) or [passkey account](/docs/accounts/create#create-a-passkey-account) within your app.
- **Your backend approves payments:** use your signing infrastructure or a [wallet provider](/docs/accounts/providers). Keep signing credentials on the server.
- **A service acts within a budget:** authorize an [access key](/docs/accounts/agents) with a spending limit, expiry, and allowed calls.

A customer logging into your application does not itself authorize an onchain payment. Your integration must also obtain the wallet signature or use a key already authorized for that operation.

## What does your application record?

Keep the customer or business identifier alongside the network, account address, and provider wallet identifier if you use one. These records connect your users to the accounts your integration reads and signs for.

Use [balances and activity](/docs/accounts/balances) to show onchain funds. For a shared account, show each customer's ledger balance rather than the account's total balance.

For deposits, [verify the actual transfer](/docs/guide/payments/accept-a-payment#verify-stablecoin-payments) before crediting a customer. Record the network, transaction hash, and log index to avoid processing the same transfer twice. For payouts, associate your payment record with its transaction and confirm the outcome before marking it paid.
