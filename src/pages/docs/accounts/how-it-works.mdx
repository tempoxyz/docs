# How it works

A Tempo account holds stablecoins, and only the account's own keys can move them.

A Tempo account has signers and can have access keys, and Tempo checks what each can do on every transaction:

- **Signers** own the account. They approve payments and any change to the account's keys. An existing wallet's account starts with one signer: its existing key.
- **Access keys** are optional. The signers authorize each one, and it makes payments on its own, but only within the limits you set, such as a daily amount or a single recipient.

No one, including Tempo, can move an account's funds any other way.

For example, say you run a payments app and give each customer a Tempo account. The customer's passkey can approve anything on its own. If they lose their phone, a backup key they keep and a recovery key your app keeps can approve together to replace it. Your app's server holds an access key that pays the customer's scheduled bills, up to 500 OUSD a day.

## The address holds the funds

<AccountDiagram
  name="address"
  alt="An address is either a single-key account (EOA) with one key, or a configurable account with weighted signers and a threshold. Both kinds have TIP-20 balances, access keys, and a fee token."
/>

The address is the account's public ID. It holds the account's stablecoins and sets its default [fee token](/docs/accounts/fees), the stablecoin it pays network fees in.

A single-key account has one key, like a standard Ethereum account (an EOA), so losing the key loses the account. A configurable account has signers instead, and its address stays the same when they change, so the customer keeps their address and balance after replacing a lost phone. You can [upgrade a single-key account](/docs/accounts/key-management/account-upgrade) to a configurable one at the same address.

Single-key accounts and their access keys work on mainnet and testnet today, including the ones in [wallets](/docs/accounts/wallets) your users already have, in your [app's embedded wallets](/docs/accounts/apps), behind your [backend automations](/docs/accounts/automations), and with [custodians](/docs/accounts/custodians). Configurable accounts, and upgrades to them, run only on the [Tempo Accounts devnet](/docs/accounts/create#connect-to-the-devnet).

## Signers approve together

<AccountDiagram
  name="signers"
  alt="Three signers with weights: a passkey at 2, and a backup key and a recovery key at 1 each, toward a threshold of 2. The passkey approves alone, or both keys approve together."
/>

Each signer is a [private key](/docs/accounts/authentication/private-keys), a [device key](/docs/accounts/authentication/device-keys), or a [passkey](/docs/accounts/authentication/passkeys), with a weight. A payment, a new access key, or a signer change goes through once the weights of the signers who approve it reach the account's [threshold](/docs/accounts/policies/signing-thresholds).

In the example, the passkey has weight 2, the backup and recovery keys have weight 1 each, and the threshold is 2. The passkey pays alone. Your recovery key alone reaches 1 of 2, so you can't move the customer's funds. If the customer loses their phone, the backup and recovery keys together [replace the passkey](/docs/accounts/apps/recover-an-account).

## Access keys pay alone

<AccountDiagram
  name="access-keys"
  alt="An access key has a key type (passkey, P-256, or secp256k1), spending limits per TIP-20 token that apply once or per period, an expiry, and scopes over contracts, functions, and recipients. An admin access key has no limits and manages other access keys."
/>

A payout server can't ask for the customer's passkey on every payout. Instead, the signers approve an [access key](/docs/accounts/access-keys) once, and from then on it pays alone within its bounds: a spending limit per stablecoin, an expiry, and scopes that limit which contracts and functions it calls and whom it pays. Tempo checks those bounds on every transaction, so even if the payout server's key leaks, it can't spend past its daily limit.

An [admin access key](/docs/accounts/access-keys/admin-access-keys) has no bounds and can manage the account's other access keys, so give one only to a party you'd trust with the whole balance. No access key counts toward the threshold, admin or not, so only the signers can change the signers or the threshold.

[Security](/docs/accounts/security) covers what Tempo enforces, what your app is responsible for, and what happens if a key is compromised.

Specifications: [configurable accounts](https://tips.sh/1107), [Account Keychain](/docs/protocol/transactions/AccountKeychain), [admin access keys](https://tips.sh/1049).
