# Evaluate stablecoin settlement

Stablecoin settlement records a token transfer on a blockchain under that network's finality rules. A complete business payment can also require funding, conversion, provider credit, or bank delivery. Use the [receiving guide](/docs/guide/payments/accept-a-payment) to verify the onchain transfer, then evaluate the other legs before marking the payment complete.

Start with the payment you need to complete. A supplier retaining a stablecoin balance, a contractor requesting a bank deposit, and a transfer between treasury accounts have different requirements. Evaluate the network within that complete workflow.

## Define what settlement must prove

Onchain settlement concerns the transfer recorded on the network. It is distinct from a request being submitted, an exchange crediting a customer account, or a payout provider delivering local currency.

Define the completion signal for every leg:

| Payment stage             | Evidence your operation needs                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Funding                   | The required funds are available to the account or provider executing the payment.                     |
| Onchain delivery          | Successful delivery of the intended token and amount to the intended recipient on the correct network. |
| Network finality          | The network's documented settlement condition has been met.                                            |
| Provider credit or payout | The provider has credited the intended customer or delivered the requested bank or other payout.       |
| Business reconciliation   | The payment is matched to its obligation and recorded once.                                            |

A single transaction hash cannot establish all five. For cross-network flows, a finalized source transaction also does not prove destination delivery. Follow the route's [delivery and refund status](/docs/routes/delivery) until the intended outcome is confirmed.

## Compare networks using the same payment

When comparing Tempo with Ethereum, Base, Solana, Tron, or another network, keep the payment requirements constant. Published fees and block times can describe different transaction types and different stages of confirmation. They are not a substitute for a complete-flow evaluation.

| Criterion               | Evidence to collect                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exact asset             | Issuer, token contract, network, redemption terms, and whether a bridge or conversion is involved.                            |
| Recipient access        | Current wallet, custodian, exchange, or payout support for that exact asset and network.                                      |
| Settlement signal       | The documented finality condition and how the application observes it, including failure or delayed-network behavior.         |
| Total operating cost    | Representative transfer and contract-call fees, fee funding, provider charges, conversion, and any payout costs.              |
| Integration             | Wallet signing, RPC and API support, contract compatibility, event handling, and the features your application actually uses. |
| Liquidity               | A current quote for the required amount and direction, available funding, and exit or redemption conditions.                  |
| Controls and operations | Approvals, token policies, reconciliation, privacy, monitoring, support, and recovery ownership.                              |

Test a representative payment and the exceptions that matter to your business. Include a rejected recipient, insufficient funds, an expired quote, and a delayed response. Record observed results and the assumptions behind them rather than publishing a permanent ranking from a point-in-time fee.

## Check finality and successful delivery together

Each network defines its own confirmation and finality stages. Compare the stage your application will rely on. A fast acknowledgment, an included transaction, a finalized block, and a bridged withdrawal can be different events.

[Tempo's consensus documentation](/docs/protocol/blockspace/consensus) specifies no re-orgs after a block is finalized. It also describes the network conditions required to keep making progress. Near-instant onchain settlement should not be interpreted as an unconditional delivery-time guarantee for every payment flow.

Finality alone does not establish that the correct payment succeeded. The [Tempo receiving guide](/docs/guide/payments/accept-a-payment) instructs applications to check the network, token contract, recipient, and amount in successful transfer events. It also distinguishes a delivered receipt from funds held by a receive policy. Match that evidence to the invoice before marking it paid.

## Model fees for the operations you will run

Budget for the actual transaction types in the application. A simple token transfer, a batch, and a contract call can consume different resources. Include any provider, custody, conversion, and payout charges when calculating the cost of the complete payment.

Tempo's [fee system](/docs/protocol/fees) charges fees in eligible stablecoins and uses a bounded dynamic base fee. No separate gas token is required. Standard stablecoin transfers have sub-cent fees; the actual fee varies with the operation and base fee.

[Fee sponsorship](/docs/guide/payments/sponsor-user-fees) can make the application responsible for network fees. Decide which account pays, which operations qualify, and how the application handles a depleted sponsor balance or an unavailable fee token. This is an operating requirement even when the recipient sees no network-fee prompt.

## Confirm liquidity and the asset at both ends

A stablecoin symbol can appear on more than one network. Verify the contract and supported deposit route, not just the name. A wallet's ability to display an address is different from a custodian or exchange accepting that asset for credit.

Review issuer and reserve information, redemption access, custody, and any bridge dependency. [Minting and burning](/docs/guide/issuance/mint-stablecoins) help explain the issuer-side lifecycle; a secondary-market transfer does not automatically give every holder direct redemption access.

If conversion is required, quote the amount and direction you need. Do not assume that two dollar-pegged tokens always exchange at par or that support for both guarantees sufficient liquidity. For Routes integrations, use the current [network and asset documentation](/docs/routes/networks) to establish candidate paths, then request the actual route quote.

If the recipient needs local currency, add the provider's eligibility, FX, beneficiary details, and bank delivery to the evaluation. [Cross-border payments](/docs/resources/guides/cross-border-payments) covers that complete route.

## Assign control and reconciliation responsibilities

The settlement layer does not decide who may approve a company payment, manage wallet recovery, or resolve a customer dispute. Assign those responsibilities to the application, custodian, issuer, and payment providers involved.

Tempo's [TIP-403 Policy Registry](/docs/protocol/tip403/overview) lets TIP-20 tokens share access-control policies. These primitives can support an issuer's control framework; they do not establish that an application has completed identity checks, screening, or other obligations.

[Payment references](/docs/guide/payments/transfer-memos) connect transfers with business references. Keep the full invoice, customer details, and sensitive records offchain. Use a reference that your internal systems can resolve, and define who can access those records.

Record the network, transaction hash, and transfer log index as a unique event key. Connect it to the provider payment ID and invoice, and update the ledger once. A matching memo or a changed balance alone is insufficient evidence to fulfill an order. See [payment reconciliation](/docs/resources/guides/payment-reconciliation) for the broader workflow.

## Move from evaluation to a payment pilot

Choose one flow with known counterparties and a clear recipient outcome. Document the expected amount, cost, completion evidence, and exception owner before executing it.

Measure time to usable funds, complete cost, failed or delayed payments, and reconciliation effort. Retain the bank and provider relationships the flow still needs. A settlement network earns its place by improving that complete operation.

## Stablecoin settlement questions

<details>
<summary>How quickly can a stablecoin payment be settled?</summary>

Timing depends on the network and the payment flow. Tempo provides near-instant onchain settlement. Funding, cross-network delivery, provider account credit, and local bank payout can be separate steps with separate completion signals.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What makes a settlement rail enterprise-ready?</summary>

Evaluate the supported assets, settlement signal, fee behavior, custody, permissions, reconciliation, liquidity, provider coverage, and recovery procedures. Test the complete payment flow with representative amounts and exceptions, rather than choosing from a headline speed or fee.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How is settlement finality different from confirmation?</summary>

A confirmation may mean that a system has accepted or observed a transaction. Finality is the network-specific point at which the recorded transaction is considered settled. Your application must also verify successful delivery of the intended asset and amount; an acknowledgment or transaction hash alone is insufficient.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can stablecoin settlement replace SWIFT or correspondent banking?</summary>

Stablecoins can provide another settlement path for supported flows. Funding, FX, and recipient delivery can still depend on banks and payment providers. Compare the complete route and its operating requirements before deciding which existing steps to retain.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How should I compare Tempo with another blockchain for payments?</summary>

Compare the same token or explicitly priced conversion, recipient endpoint, transaction type, and operating assumptions. Evaluate actual integration support, finality signals, total fees, liquidity, controls, and delivery evidence. A shared token symbol or an advertised block time is not a complete comparison.

</details>
