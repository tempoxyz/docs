# Blockchain payment workflows

A blockchain payment uses an onchain asset transfer as part of a payment workflow. The network records the transfer; your application and providers supply funding, signing, customer records, and any local-currency payout. Start with the asset and account your recipient needs, then map the components that deliver it.

## Define the recipient outcome

A supported token in a wallet, a provider account balance, and local currency in a bank account are different results. Specify the network, exact token contract, recipient account, and usable amount before selecting infrastructure.

Stablecoins are tokens designed to track a reference value, often the U.S. dollar. That design can help denominate payments, but does not remove issuer, redemption, custody, or conversion exposure. Verify the exact token representation and supported network; similar tickers do not establish compatibility. Use the [stablecoin registry](/docs/partners/stablecoins) when choosing an asset on Tempo.

## Map the payment lifecycle

| Stage | Integration responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Funding | Make the supported payment asset and network fee funding available. |
| Authorization | Apply business approvals and the account's signing permissions. |
| Submission | Save the intended payment and transaction identifier before handling retries. |
| Onchain delivery | Verify the successful transaction and expected delivered token transfer. |
| Additional payout | Confirm any provider credit, currency conversion, or bank delivery separately. |
| Reconciliation | Match the transfer and delivery records to the business operation once. |

A transaction hash identifies a transaction. It does not alone prove that the call succeeded, the recipient received the expected token, or a bank payout completed.

## Implement payment delivery checks

:::::steps

### Validate the payment instruction

Record the network, token contract, recipient, amount in base units, and business reference. Apply your application's approvals and check the available token balance and fee funding. Follow [sending payments](/docs/guide/payments/send-a-payment).

### Submit and preserve the attempt

Store the transaction identifier against the payment record. If submission times out, investigate that attempt before creating another. A repeated application request should not create a duplicate payment.

### Verify the delivered transfer

Follow [receiving payments](/docs/guide/payments/accept-a-payment) to check the successful transaction and the expected transfer's network, contract, recipient, and amount. If a receive policy holds funds, the intended receiver has not received the payment even though the call succeeds.

### Credit the business record once

Use the network, transaction hash, and transfer log index as a unique event key. Save the event and update the invoice or customer ledger atomically. Recover missed events after a service reconnects.

:::::

## Assemble the account and operating model

Choose the wallet or custodian, key management, account recovery, and signing policy for the flow. A platform API credential and an account signing key confer different authority. The [account integration guide](/docs/accounts/use-cases) explains the model.

Use [access keys](/docs/accounts/access-keys) when a device, service, or agent needs bounded signing authority. Apply token spending limits, allowed calls, and expiry. Confirm the account type and network availability before deploying an integration.

Assign operational responsibilities for identity checks, screening, monitoring, and exception handling. [Token and receive controls](/docs/resources/guides/compliance-controls) enforce configured technical policies; they do not perform every surrounding workflow.

## Use Tempo's payment capabilities

- **Stablecoin fees:** pay network fees in eligible USD-denominated TIP-20 tokens under the [fee rules](/docs/resources/guides/stablecoin-fees).
- **Fee sponsorship:** let an application [pay the network fee](/docs/guide/payments/sponsor-user-fees) while a customer authorizes the payment.
- **Payment references:** use [32-byte memos](/docs/guide/payments/transfer-memos) to connect transfers to separately stored business records.
- **Transfer controls:** use [TIP-20 roles and TIP-403 policies](/docs/resources/guides/compliance-controls) for issuer-defined token rules. Configure [account receive policies](/docs/accounts/policies/receive-policies) separately to control incoming payments.
- **Private account activity:** evaluate [Tempo Zones](/docs/zones) and their current availability. A Zone's operator can read its ledger, and deposits and withdrawals leave public records. See [Zone privacy](/docs/zones/privacy).

These capabilities describe the onchain integration. Funding, conversion, and bank-delivery timing still depend on the providers and route used.

## Compare complete payment routes

Include funding, custody, network fees, conversion, recipient delivery, and exception handling in a route comparison. Bank systems include scheduled payments, wires, and around-the-clock services; treating every bank transfer as slow gives an incomplete comparison.

Use [payment rail comparisons](/docs/resources/guides/payment-rails) for evaluation, [corporate treasury operations](/docs/resources/guides/corporate-treasury) for intercompany funding, or [remittance operations](/docs/resources/guides/remittances) for recipient payouts.

## Blockchain payment questions

### Does a customer need to hold stablecoins directly?

A provider can handle stablecoins between fiat collection and delivery where its product supports the route. A direct onchain integration needs supported token balances and signing. Confirm custody and provider responsibilities for the selected model.

### Is blockchain payment data public?

Public-chain activity is generally visible. Do not put customer details into public payment references. For a Zone, assess the operator's access and the public deposit and withdrawal boundary separately.

### Does successful execution prove payment delivery?

No. Verify the expected delivered transfer. A receive policy can redirect a successful inbound call to a guard-held receipt. Additional provider or bank delivery requires its own evidence.
