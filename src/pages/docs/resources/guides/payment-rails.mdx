# Compare payment rails

A payment rail is the infrastructure used to move or settle value between accounts. ACH, wires, instant bank payments, and blockchains supply different parts of that process; SWIFT supplies financial messaging. Compare the complete funding, settlement, and recipient-delivery flow for the outcome you need.

## Separate the legs of a payment

A business can use several rails for one payment. An ACH credit can fund an account, a stablecoin transfer can move value between providers, and a local bank payment can deliver the recipient's currency.

Record the source account and currency, the asset used between providers, and the recipient account and currency. Specify which organization confirms each stage. A message, network settlement, provider balance credit, and recipient bank deposit are different events.

## Compare the main rail types

| Option | Role | Integration checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ACH | U.S. bank-account credits and debits, including scheduled and recurring payments. | Account validation, debit authorization where applicable, deadlines, settlement windows, returns, and charges. |
| Domestic wire | A bank payment using a service such as Fedwire. | Participating banks, customer cutoffs, beneficiary details, fees, and evidence of recipient credit. |
| Domestic instant payment | Around-the-clock payments through participating institutions, such as FedNow. | Availability for both accounts, product limits, fraud controls, pricing, and recipient access. |
| SWIFT-enabled international payment | Financial messages coordinate a payment between institutions. | Currency route, banks and providers moving funds, deductions, FX, tracking, and beneficiary delivery. |
| Stablecoin payment | A specified token moves between supported accounts on a blockchain. | Network and token support, custody, signing, fee funding, delivery evidence, conversion, and recipient access. |

[Nacha documents Same Day ACH and scheduled processing](https://www.nacha.org/content/ach-payments-fact-sheet). [Fedwire provides real-time gross settlement](https://www.frbservices.org/financial-services/fedwire-funds-service/). [FedNow operates around the clock through participating institutions](https://www.frbservices.org/financial-services/fednow-service/about-fednow-service/). [SWIFT describes its financial messaging role](https://www.swift.com/about-us/who-we-are/what-swift).

These are system capabilities. Your bank or provider sets the access conditions, product limits, pricing, and operating procedures for your account.

## Read settlement timing and netting terms

### Real-time gross settlement and net settlement

Real-time gross settlement (RTGS) processes payments individually as they arrive, rather than settling only the difference between amounts owed. For example, the [Federal Reserve describes Fedwire](https://www.frbservices.org/wp-content/uploads/funds.pdf) as processing each payment order individually and, when accepted, settling to the receiving participant's Federal Reserve account in central bank money.

In [deferred net settlement](https://www.bis.org/publications/innovations-payments), obligations are combined and the net positions settle at scheduled times. A batch can contain many payment instructions while the settlement system moves a smaller set of net amounts. An application's batch of blockchain calls is a different concept.

### T+0, T+1, and T+2 settlement

`T` identifies the transaction or trade date. `T+1` means one business day later; `T+2` means two. A Friday `T+2` settlement normally falls on Tuesday if Monday is a business day. Check the relevant calendar and cutoff before promising a recipient date.

`T+0` means same-day settlement, which can still involve a processing window before the recipient has access.

## Match the rail to the payment workflow

ACH can fit predictable domestic credits or authorized recurring debits. Sending a credit and collecting a debit require different application and authorization flows. Same Day ACH uses banking-day settlement windows; it is different from an instant payment service.

A wire can fit a time-sensitive bank payment when the banks, deadlines, and beneficiary requirements fit. Interbank settlement and credit to the customer's account are separate events.

For an out-of-hours domestic payment, evaluate available instant bank services alongside stablecoins. Both accounts must support the service.

A stablecoin route can fit a recipient that accepts the token, or a provider that converts and delivers it. Receiving a dollar-denominated token and receiving local currency in a bank account are different outcomes.

## Compare the same amount and recipient outcome

Hold the funding method, source currency, recipient currency, and delivery method constant. Decide whether you are fixing the sender's total budget or the recipient's net amount.

| Comparison field | Record for each route |
| --- | --- |
| Sender funding | Total debit, including charges outside the payment amount. |
| Recipient outcome | Net amount, currency, account type, and delivery method. |
| FX terms | Executable rate, conversion charges, and quote expiry. |
| Delivery | Expected completion and evidence for each payment stage. |
| Exceptions | Failed-payment, return, investigation, support, and recovery costs. |
| Liquidity | Required balances, replenishment time, and withdrawal access. |

For example, if a supplier expects a local-currency bank deposit, compare a bank deposit for every route. The network fee is only one part of that cost. Use the [cross-border payment guide](/docs/resources/guides/cross-border-payments) to document the route.

## Compare services at the same layer

A payment service may bundle funding, recipient lookup, currency conversion, account records, support, and delivery. A blockchain network supplies a different part of the flow.

Before replacing a service, list the features your application needs: bank or wallet delivery, signing and recovery, identity checks, invoices, recurring collections, accounting records, refunds, and error handling. Then identify which component of the proposed route supplies each one.

A settlement network doesn't provide the complete customer product. Check a payment provider's current account and country support.

## Define returns and refunds separately from settlement

Settlement finality describes when a system considers a transfer settled. Provider policies and customer rights for the underlying purchase are separate.

For onchain payments, define the successful delivery event and the network's completion rule. Then specify how to handle a mistaken beneficiary, held receipt, refund, or disputed purchase. A refund can require a new payment and separate business approval.

For bank payments, follow the applicable return and investigation process. A single “reversible” label can hide differences between the rail, account product, and reason for a return.

## Payment rail questions

### Can stablecoins replace ACH?

The choice depends on the payment. ACH supports bank-account credits and debits. Stablecoins provide an onchain option when the asset, recipient, funding, and payout services are supported. Compare the complete workflow for the payment you need to make.

### Is SWIFT a settlement network?

SWIFT provides financial messaging between institutions. Banks and providers move and credit the funds. A SWIFT message, interbank settlement, and recipient account credit are separate events.

### Are stablecoin payments always cheaper?

No. Obtain quotes for the same funding, conversion, and recipient delivery. A small network fee can be one component of a larger payment cost.
