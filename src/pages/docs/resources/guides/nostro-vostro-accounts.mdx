# Nostro and vostro accounts

A nostro account is an account a bank holds with another bank, commonly to make and receive payments in a foreign currency. Vostro describes that same account from the bank holding the funds. They are two perspectives on one banking relationship.

For a payments team, the important questions are who holds the funds, who can move them, and how much liquidity must be available before a payment can complete.

## One account viewed from two banks

Suppose Bank A holds a dollar account at Bank B. Bank A calls it its nostro account, meaning our account with you. Bank B calls it a vostro account, meaning your account with us. Bank A records a claim on Bank B; Bank B records its obligation to Bank A.

| Perspective                         | Name   | Operational question                                                    |
| ----------------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bank A, which owns the balance      | Nostro | Do we have enough available dollars to fund the payment?                |
| Bank B, which maintains the account | Vostro | Can we execute this customer's instruction under the account agreement? |

The [ECB's explanation of correspondent banking](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/ecb.eleventhsurveycorrespondentbankingeuro202011~c280262151.en.pdf#page=6) describes these two perspectives on the account relationship. The account provides access to services; its agreement and the payment route determine the speed and fees.

## How an account supports a payment

In a simplified example, Bank A instructs Bank B to use its dollar balance to pay a beneficiary. Bank B debits the account and arranges the receiving leg. Additional institutions, currency conversion, and local payment systems may be involved.

An instruction sent successfully is different from a beneficiary receiving funds. Operations teams should retain the instruction identifier, account entry, fees, any FX trade, and final delivery confirmation. See the broader [payment rails comparison](/docs/resources/guides/payment-rails) for how messaging, clearing, and settlement differ.

## Why prefunding matters

Holding a nostro account and prefunding it are different decisions. The account is the arrangement; [prefunding](/docs/resources/guides/prefunding) is putting liquidity in place before payments need it. Available credit, incoming payments, and the service agreement can also affect funding needs.

For an illustrative payout program, a team might track three figures separately: the account balance, the amount already committed to pending payouts, and the amount immediately available for new instructions. A large balance can still conceal a shortage if most of it is reserved.

Replenishment cutoffs, holidays, currency exposure, and access to credit determine how much buffer a particular program needs. A count of destination countries alone does not determine the number or size of accounts required.

## What stablecoin settlement changes

Stablecoin transfers can provide another way to move value between participating providers. Tempo supports near-instant onchain settlement around the clock, so an operator can evaluate whether faster replenishment reduces balances held ahead of demand.

That benefit depends on the complete flow. A provider paying recipients in local currency still needs conversion liquidity, a supported payout method, and confirmation from the receiving provider. The bank account may remain part of that flow. A stablecoin balance also carries different issuer, custody, and redemption considerations from a bank deposit.

Use the [cross-border payments guide](/docs/resources/guides/cross-border-payments) to assess the full payment and the [reconciliation guide](/docs/resources/guides/payment-reconciliation) to connect its records.

## Nostro and vostro accounts FAQ

<details>
<summary>Are nostro and vostro accounts separate accounts?</summary>

They can describe the same account from opposite sides. The bank holding money at another bank calls it a nostro; the servicing bank calls it a vostro.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a nostro account the same as prefunding?</summary>

No. Nostro describes an account relationship. Prefunding describes placing liquidity ahead of expected payments, which can happen in several types of account or provider arrangement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do stablecoins eliminate correspondent banking?</summary>

They can replace or complement parts of a settlement flow. Fiat collection, currency conversion, bank payouts, and issuer redemption can still depend on banking relationships.

</details>
