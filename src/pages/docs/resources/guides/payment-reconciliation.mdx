# Plan payment reconciliation

Stablecoin payment reconciliation matches verified token transfers to invoices, payables, treasury movements, and finance entries. Use [Receive payments](/docs/guide/payments/accept-a-payment) to verify delivery and [Payment references](/docs/guide/payments/transfer-memos) to encode and find the business reference.

A confirmed onchain transfer is settlement evidence, not a journal entry. Your integration supplies the business context and accounting mapping. Keep your existing finance controls and introduce onchain activity as another settlement source.

## Assign a role to each finance system

| Layer | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Digital-asset subledger | Map transfers to business events, preserve settlement evidence, and prepare entries for the chart of accounts. |
| Treasury system | Track usable balances and internal transfers across entities, accounts, custodians, and networks. |
| ERP or accounting system | Apply approved accounting rules, maintain invoice balances, and record financial outcomes. |
| Event ingestion | Read transfer activity, recover missed events, prevent duplicate processing, and expose exceptions. |

One provider may supply several layers. Confirm the actual connector, fields, supported networks, and ownership of the ERP mapping. Protocol-level payment references do not constitute a built-in ERP integration.

## Define the records before moving funds

Reconciliation connects a verified transfer to a business obligation. Define that connection before sending payment instructions or releasing a vendor run. The following is an implementation pattern for your finance system, not a built-in ERP connector.

| Record               | Keep in your application                                                           |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business obligation  | Invoice or payout ID, customer or vendor, legal entity, and expected amount.       |
| Payment instructions | Network, token contract, recipient, and the requested reference.                   |
| Settlement evidence  | Network, token contract, transaction hash, transfer log index, delivered amount, and confirmation status. |
| Accounting outcome   | Amount applied, remaining balance, fees, ERP record ID, and unresolved exceptions. |

Use a short reference in the [32-byte transfer memo](/docs/guide/payments/transfer-memos) and retain the rest in your database. Customer details, purchase orders, and GL codes do not need to appear in public transaction data. A shared transaction hash can contain several transfers, so the hash alone is not a unique payment identifier.

### Apply incoming payments to receivables

Consider an illustrative invoice for 1,000 USDC.e. A payment carrying its reference is a candidate match. Before crediting the invoice, verify the network, token contract, recipient, amount, and successful delivery using the [receiving guide](/docs/guide/payments/accept-a-payment). If only 600 arrives, apply it as a partial payment or send it for review according to your policy; do not silently mark the invoice paid.

Store the network, transaction hash, and transfer log index as a unique event key. Save that event and its invoice update together. This prevents a repeated webhook or replayed event from applying the same payment twice. After an interruption, read historical activity and continue through pagination to recover missed events.

### Close the loop on payables

For an approved supplier run, preserve each vendor's invoice ID, authorized destination, and expected amount before submission. Assign a distinct reference to each transfer, even when several transfers share one batch transaction. Match the receipt's delivered transfers back to those records before marking invoices paid.

Track network fees separately from invoice principal. If the recipient needs local currency, retain the payout provider's delivery reference and status as well: the onchain transfer does not prove that the vendor's bank account was credited. See the [batch payment workflow](/docs/resources/guides/batch-payments) for validation and release controls.

### Make exceptions visible

Maintain a queue for unmatched references, wrong assets, partial or excess payments, blocked receipts, and refunds. Keep the original evidence when a reviewer resolves an exception. Link a refund to the original receivable and record its own confirmed transfer; changing the invoice status does not send money back.

At close, compare expected payments, confirmed transfers, and posted ERP records. Investigate differences rather than treating a wallet's total balance as proof that individual invoices have been paid.

## Connect events to finance entries

Treat a memo as a candidate match, then connect the verified transfer to the finance entry. Keep invoice contents, customer information, purchase orders, and GL codes offchain.

Connectors can synchronize through event-driven updates, polling, or scheduled batches. Whichever approach you use, retain a checkpoint for historical recovery and make retries safe. Read [account activity](/docs/api/activities), [transfers](/docs/api/transfers), and [API pagination](/docs/api/pagination) for the maintained API surface.

Your mapping should distinguish customer receipts, supplier payments, internal transfers, conversion, and fees. A transfer between two company accounts is not automatically revenue or an expense. Record the entity that owns the balance and preserve the provider's records when a custodian or payout service participates.

## Keep settlement evidence and accounting outcomes separate

Once a block is finalized on Tempo, it is not reversed by chain reorganizations. Read the [consensus documentation](/docs/protocol/blockspace/consensus) for the network assumptions. This does not eliminate operational errors, account restrictions, or the need to verify that the intended transfer succeeded.

[Network fees](/docs/protocol/fees) use eligible stablecoins and a bounded dynamic base fee. Record the actual fee separately from principal. Standard stablecoin transfers have sub-cent fees, but contract calls, batches, provider charges, conversion, and bank delivery affect the complete payment cost.

At close, reconcile opening balances, verified transfers, conversions, fees, and closing balances with the subledger and ERP. Keep unresolved items visible rather than making the wallet balance stand in for invoice-level evidence.

## Reconciliation questions

### How does stablecoin reconciliation differ from bank reconciliation?

The obligation-matching process is similar. The settlement source changes to onchain events, which need network, token, recipient, amount, and event identifiers. Your application adds the invoice and accounting context.

### Do stablecoins replace treasury or ERP systems?

No. The onchain records flow into the systems finance uses for cash management, financial controls, and reporting.

### Can a successful transaction still leave an invoice unpaid?

Yes. A successful transaction can deliver the wrong asset or amount, or a receive policy can hold funds rather than credit the intended recipient. Verify the expected delivered transfer before applying it to the invoice. A provider's fiat payout needs separate completion evidence.

### Can an existing ERP or TMS handle the activity?

Evaluate the specific connector or API mapping. Confirm its support for the intended network, assets, event identifiers, historical recovery, and exceptions. Structured references help that integration; they do not create it automatically.
