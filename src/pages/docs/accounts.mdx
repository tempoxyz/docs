# Accounts

Create accounts to hold stablecoins and send payments. Choose who controls signing, then grant services or agents the access they need.

[Create an account →](/docs/accounts/create)

</DocsProductOverview>

An account holds stablecoin balances at an address. A wallet is software or a service that manages signing keys and requests signatures.

## Fit accounts into your application

Your customer record and a Tempo account serve different purposes. Your application identifies the customer and maintains its ledger; the Tempo account holds token balances and authorizes transactions. Associate addresses with the customer or business records they serve.

| Component | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Your application and backend | Customer identity, address mappings, payment records, and reconciliation. |
| Your signer or wallet provider | Key storage, signature requests, and the authentication and recovery flow you choose. |
| Tempo network | Validate transaction signatures and permissions, execute transactions, and record token balances and transfers. |
| Tempo API | Read indexed balances and activity and deliver configured webhook events. API credentials do not authorize account spending. |

Follow the [integration walkthrough](/docs/accounts/integration) to connect these pieces, from creating an account to recording its first payment.

## Choose who controls signing

| Who authorizes transactions? | Integration path |
| --- | --- |
| Your backend, for payouts or treasury operations | [Create an account](/docs/accounts/create#create-an-account-from-a-signing-key) using your signing infrastructure, or [use a wallet provider](/docs/accounts/providers). |
| A customer, from inside your application | Use a [wallet provider](/docs/accounts/providers) for embedded wallets, or create a [passkey account](/docs/accounts/create#create-a-passkey-account). |
| A customer, through an existing wallet | [Connect a wallet](/docs/accounts/integrate) and request authorization for your application. |

For a provider integration, decide who can request signatures and how users regain access. Compare the provider's authentication, recovery, key export, and signing policies against those requirements.

If you collect customer deposits into a shared balance, [customer deposit addresses](/docs/guide/payments/virtual-addresses) give each customer a virtual address that forwards funds to your account. Track each customer's balance in your ledger; virtual addresses do not hold separate balances.

## Delegate access to applications and services

Use a limited access key when a service, customer session, or agent needs to act on an account. Set its expiry, token budget, and allowed calls for the task. For example, a payout service can receive permission to transfer one stablecoin to approved recipients within a budget.

[Keys and signing](/docs/accounts/keys) explains root authority, delegation, and how transaction signing differs from customer login and API authentication.

- [Access keys](/docs/accounts/access-keys) covers authorization, limits, and revocation.
- [Give an agent access](/docs/accounts/agents) explains how to delegate a defined task.
- [Manage admin keys](/docs/accounts/admin-keys) covers delegation of key management itself.

Plan recovery with your wallet or signing provider. The root public key determines the account address; adding or revoking an access key does not replace it.

## Understand account visibility

Balances and transfers on Tempo's public ledger are publicly readable. Signing permissions control who can move funds; they do not make the account's activity private. For private balances and activity, review [Tempo Zones](/docs/zones/privacy), including what remains visible to the operator and on the public ledger.
