# Accounts

A Tempo account is a stablecoin address with its rules built in: which keys approve payments, and how much a server or agent can spend on its own. Tempo checks those rules on every transaction and can't override them, so your customers hold their own funds.

[How it works →](/docs/accounts/how-it-works)

</DocsProductOverview>

:::warning
Tempo Accounts is in early development. Configurable accounts, with several signers and a threshold, run only on the [Tempo Accounts devnet](/docs/accounts/create#connect-to-the-devnet), not on mainnet or testnet. [Contact Tempo](https://tempo.xyz/contact) to discuss access.
:::

## What's in a Tempo account

- **Address:** holds the account's stablecoins and never changes, even when you [replace its signers](/docs/accounts/key-management).
- **Signers:** approve payments and changes to the account. Each is a [private key, device key, passkey, or OIDC login](/docs/accounts/authentication) with a weight. A transaction runs once its approvers' weights add up to the [threshold](/docs/accounts/policies/signing-thresholds), such as any 2 of 3.
- **Access keys:** let a customer's phone, your backend, or an AI agent [pay on its own](/docs/accounts/access-keys), within the spending limits, expiry, and allowed calls you set. A leaked key still can't spend past them.

Each transaction pays a network fee in a USD stablecoin, not a separate gas token, and a [fee payer](/docs/accounts/fees) can pay it for your customers. On mainnet and testnet today, use a single-key account (EOA), like a standard Ethereum account: one key, such as a passkey, approves its payments and authorizes its access keys. On the Tempo Accounts devnet, a single-key account can also [upgrade in place](/docs/accounts/key-management/account-upgrade) to signers and a threshold, and keep its address and balance.
