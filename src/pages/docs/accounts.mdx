# Accounts

Set up accounts to hold stablecoins and authorize payments from your application. Choose whether your service or your customer controls signing.

[Create an account →](/docs/accounts/create)

</DocsProductOverview>

An account holds stablecoin balances at an address. A wallet or signing service manages the keys that authorize transactions. Keep customer, invoice, and payroll records in your application, and associate the relevant account addresses with those records.

## Choose an account model

| Who authorizes transactions? | Integration path |
| --- | --- |
| Your backend, for payouts or treasury operations | [Create an account](/docs/accounts/create#create-an-account-from-a-signing-key) using your signing infrastructure, or [use a wallet provider](/docs/accounts/providers). |
| A customer, from inside your application | Use a [wallet provider](/docs/accounts/providers) for embedded wallets, or create a [passkey account](/docs/accounts/create#create-a-passkey-account). |
| A customer, through an existing wallet | [Connect a wallet](/docs/accounts/integrate) and request authorization for your application. |

For a provider integration, decide who can request signatures and how users regain access. Compare the provider's authentication, recovery, key export, and signing policies against those requirements.

If you collect customer deposits into a shared balance, [virtual addresses](/docs/guide/payments/virtual-addresses) give each customer a deposit address that forwards funds to your account. Track each customer's balance in your ledger; virtual addresses do not hold separate balances.

## Delegate access to applications and services

The **root key** is the account's original signing authority. Authorize separate **limited access keys** for a customer session, backend service, or agent, with an expiry, token spending limit, and allowed calls. Each key acts on the same account and can be revoked independently.

For example, give a payout service permission to transfer a specific stablecoin to approved recipients within a budget. A customer can authorize a short-lived session to make payments without confirming each transaction. Your application's sign-in flow identifies the customer; access-key permissions control what the session can do onchain.

- [Access keys](/docs/accounts/access-keys) covers authorization, limits, and revocation.
- [Give an agent access](/docs/accounts/agents) explains how to delegate a defined task.
- [Manage admin keys](/docs/accounts/admin-keys) covers delegation of key management itself.

Plan recovery with your wallet or signing provider. The root public key determines the account address; adding or revoking an access key does not replace it.
